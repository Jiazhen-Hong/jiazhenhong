Jiazhen Hong is an AI Research Scientist at EMOTIV, where he leads research on EEG foundation models that generalize across recording devices and tasks. He also leads the development of ChatBCI, a real-time brain-computer interface that uses large language models to help people communicate through spelling. His broader research spans multimodal learning, human-machine interfaces, and AI for healthcare and cognitive aging.

He represents EMOTIV in research discussions with Nike Research and Meta Brain. At Meta’s Open Source AI for Science Summit, he presented EEG-X and its NeuralBench results and demonstrated real-time EEG spelling. He also hosted EMOTIV’s live EEG demo and Q&A at NeuroNight SF.

He earned his Ph.D. in Computer Engineering from Rutgers University and his M.Sc. from Stevens Institute of Technology, and holds bachelor's degrees from Jimei University and Chung Yuan Christian University. His work has appeared in Scientific Reports, IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering, and IEEE Transactions on Knowledge and Data Engineering. Before Rutgers, he conducted machine learning and biostatistics research for early cancer detection at Harvard Medical School.
